How to turn off YouTube autoplay for kids

It takes about thirty seconds, it costs nothing, and of everything on this site it is the change we would tell a parent to make first.

Last updated 15 August 2026 · iPhone, iPad, YouTube Kids and the web

When parents describe how a child ended up watching something upsetting, the story is almost never "they searched for it". It is: they picked one ordinary video, and then it kept going. Twenty minutes later the thing playing has no relationship to the thing they chose.

That is autoplay. It is on by default on every surface, and switching it off is the single highest-value free change available to you. Here is how, everywhere it matters.

1. The YouTube app on iPhone or iPad

  1. Open the YouTube app and play any video.
  2. Tap the video once so the player controls appear.
  3. In the top row of the player, find the autoplay toggle — a small switch, usually left of the closed-captions and gear icons.
  4. Tap it until it reads Autoplay is off.

The setting saves to whichever account is signed in. If the child watches signed out, it saves to that device instead.

2. YouTube Kids — a completely separate switch

This is the step most people miss. YouTube Kids does not inherit the setting from the main app; it has its own, and it is on by default.

  1. Open YouTube Kids and tap the lock icon in the corner.
  2. Complete the parent check, then open Settings.
  3. Select your child's profile.
  4. Turn Autoplay off.

3. YouTube in Safari or Chrome

If the child watches on the web rather than in the app, the toggle is in the same place — in the player controls, next to the settings gear. Set it per browser, and remember that a private/incognito window will not keep it.

4. Supervised accounts and Family Link

A supervised Google account does not currently expose a permanent parent-side autoplay lock. You still set it in the player as above. What Family Link does give you is the content-level setting (Explore, Explore More, Most of YouTube) and the ability to disable search — both worth setting at the same time.

While you are in there — two more free changes

Hide the comments. Under a completely ordinary gaming or toy video, the comments are frequently the least suitable thing on the page. On a supervised account they are already restricted; on a normal account, teach the child to not scroll past the video, or use a browser that hides them.

Set a daily limit. On iOS: Settings → Screen Time → App Limits. Limits work far better than repeatedly telling a child to stop, because the device says no instead of you.

What turning off autoplay does not fix

Being straight about this matters, because the gap is where parents get caught out.

Autoplay offStill happening
Videos no longer start on their own after the one they picked.The home feed and the sidebar still recommend whatever they were going to recommend. The child can still tap.
Long unsupervised drift sessions mostly stop.Shorts are a separate surface with its own endless scroll. Autoplay-off does not apply to it.
Works on the device you set it on.Not set on the tablet, the TV app, a friend's phone, or a browser you forgot.
Nothing about the content of any single video.A video the child deliberately chooses is entirely unfiltered.

So: autoplay off removes the mechanism that produces the worst sessions. It does not judge any individual video, and it is trivially reversible by the child.

How to check it actually stuck

Play a short video on the child's device and let it end without touching anything. If the screen shows a replay button and stops, it worked. If a countdown appears and the next video starts, the toggle is still on — most often because it was set on your phone rather than theirs, or on the account rather than the one they use.

Worth re-checking every month or so. The toggle lives in the player with no PIN on it, so an older child can turn it back on in one tap, sometimes without meaning to.

Common questions

Why is autoplay on by default at all?

Watch time. Autoplay exists to keep a session going, and it is very good at it. That is not a conspiracy — it is simply an objective that does not happen to be the same as yours.

My child watches on a shared iPad. Does the setting follow the account or the device?

The account, when signed in. On a shared device where everyone uses one login, changing it for the child changes it for everyone — usually fine, occasionally the reason it gets switched back.

Does this work on Android?

Yes, identically. The toggle is in the same position in the player, and YouTube Kids has the same separate setting.

What about the TV app?

Same idea, different location: on smart TVs and streaming boxes the autoplay setting is in the player's settings menu rather than on the player bar. It is a separate setting again, and on by default again.
